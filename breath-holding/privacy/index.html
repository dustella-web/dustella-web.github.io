English

Dustella (“we”) provides the Android app Breath-holding (com.contest.breath_holding). This policy explains the information handled by the app and its third-party services. Privacy contact: codenum404@gmail.com.

No account is required. Your saved routines and session records stay in the app’s private storage on your device; we do not operate a server that receives them. Google advertising services process certain device and usage data separately, as described below.

1. App information stored on your device

  • Session identifiers, start/end times, local dates and time offsets, planned breathing durations and rounds, actual elapsed and phase times, completion/interruption status and reason, and a snapshot of the routine used.
  • Routine names you enter, purpose categories, timing settings and favorites.
  • Safety-notice acknowledgment, sound, vibration, voice, theme and language preferences, recent settings, and reminder times, weekdays and scheduling/delivery state.

These data provide routines, history, weekly summaries, interruption handling and saved preferences. The app does not send routine names, session identifiers or session performance to our own server or use them as advertising targeting parameters. It does not include a separate Firebase Analytics integration.

2. Breathing and manual breath-hold timer

The app provides guided breathing without breath holds and a separate manual breath-hold timer. It does not measure actual breathing, blood oxygen or health conditions through sensors. Manual timer elapsed/recovery times and condition confirmations exist only in the current screen/process; they are not saved to the session database, CSV exports or weekly statistics.

The app is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent disease. The manual breath-hold feature requires confirmation of adult use and appropriate training and supervision conditions. These confirmations do not verify age, qualifications or supervision.

3. Google advertising and consent services

The app includes Google Mobile Ads (AdMob) and Google’s User Messaging Platform (UMP). A banner can appear on the history screen when records exist and the app is in the foreground, subject to SDK consent requirements. Core features do not require watching an ad or accepting personalized advertising.

Google’s advertising SDK automatically collects and shares IP addresses (which can estimate general location), app/product interactions, diagnostic information and device/account identifiers, including the Android advertising ID where available. Google uses these for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. Google states that SDK data are encrypted in transit using TLS. These practices are separate from the device-only session records.

Where required and available for the region, Google consent forms request privacy choices. When UMP requires a privacy-options entry point, it appears in app settings. You can also manage advertising identifiers and advertising privacy controls in Android settings. Google controls its own service retention; deleting app history does not delete information already processed by Google. Google may process information on servers outside your country under its policies.

  • Google Privacy Policy
  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services
  • Google Mobile Ads data disclosure

4. Optional notifications and voice

Reminders are off by default. If you enable and save a reminder, the app requests notification permission when required. Declining does not block breathing or history. Quiet, generic reminders open the app without automatically starting a session. Boot and time-zone changes can reschedule your chosen reminder.

Optional voice guidance uses an installed offline text-to-speech voice and sends phase names to that engine, without routine names or session details. The engine provider’s own policy applies. The widget opens routine setup without displaying personal routine names or history. The app does not request camera, microphone, contacts or precise-location access.

5. Exports and sharing

Exports happen only when you choose them. CSV files contain session dates/times, routine names and completion/interruption summaries. PNG sharing cards contain aggregate counts, practice days and elapsed time, with optional week dates, without routine names or detailed session records.

You choose a destination in Android’s document picker or share sheet. Receiving apps, cloud providers and recipients handle these copies under their own policies. Deleting app records does not remove copies you exported or shared.

6. Retention, deletion and security

Local records remain until you delete them or remove app data. Delete one record in History, or all records in Settings. Deleting all records also resets related introductory-course progress, while saved routines and preferences remain; delete saved routines separately. Clearing the app’s storage or uninstalling removes app-private local data.

Temporary PNG share files older than 24 hours are cleaned up when preparing a subsequent share. Successful individual/all-record deletion also clears temporary share files. Android may clear cached files earlier. Exported/shared copies are outside this cleanup.

The app uses Android app-private storage and excludes its record/settings storage from Android backup and device-transfer rules. It does not offer separate database or end-to-end encryption. Protect access to your device and review destinations before exporting.

7. Support, your choices and changes

If you email us, we receive your email address and whatever you include. Use only information needed for your request; app records are not automatically attached. We use correspondence to respond, resolve requests and meet applicable legal obligations, and retain it only as needed for those purposes. Support email is not used for advertising targeting.

For access, correction or deletion requests concerning support correspondence, or questions about this policy, email codenum404@gmail.com. We cannot remotely retrieve or delete records stored only on your device; use the in-app controls. Depending on your location, applicable law may provide additional rights or a right to complain to your supervisory authority. Requests concerning Google’s processing can also be made through Google’s privacy controls.

We will publish changes here and update the effective date. This policy page is hosted on GitHub Pages; visiting it is subject to the GitHub Privacy Statement.
